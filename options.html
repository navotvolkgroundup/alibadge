AliBadge

Finds the AliExpress listing a dropship store is reselling.

checking…

What gets sent, and where

  • The product photo of Shopify product pages you visit — to AliExpress.
  • Nothing else. No page text, no URLs, no browsing history, no identifiers.
  • There is no AliBadge server. Nothing is sent to the author. Results are cached in your own browser for 7 days and never leave it.
  • Requests are anonymous. Sign-in is not used and no account is required.

What it will and will not tell you

  • A number appears only when the photo is a near-exact match and the store carries importer plumbing (DSers or CJ SKU and filename shapes). Both must hold, because a matching photo alone cannot say who copied whom.
  • Otherwise you get a link to the AliExpress search and no percentage.
  • Measured on a 40-product labelled set: 0 false positives across 32 legitimate-brand products, 2 of 8 dropship products detected. It is silent far more often than it speaks, deliberately.
  • It is not a counterfeit detector and not a judgement about any merchant.

Known limits

  • An AliExpress seller who steals a brand's photograph looks identical to a dropshipper reusing a supplier's. The importer check exists to cover that, and it is not perfect.
  • Markup excludes shipping, and uses the dearest variant of the matched listing when available — the conservative end.
  • Ad blockers may block the marketplace image CDN. When images cannot be checked, AliBadge stays silent rather than guessing.
